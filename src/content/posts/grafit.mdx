## Origins

It all started in a **classroom**. In the final year of our Software Engineering Master's, an **Entrepreneurship and Innovation** course challenged us to come up with a startup idea and build a prototype. A wardrobe app had been floating around **Carlos's** mind for a while, and this was the perfect excuse to bring it to life. Between us, we had development, design and business strategy covered, so we set out to build something fun.

![](/assets/grafit/bootcamp.webp)

The *just-for-fun* part didn't last long. Through the university's partnership with Startup Braga, we joined the [Startup Point](https://startuppoint.startupbraga.com/) competition, which gave us *mentorship*, *resources*, *exposure* to industry experts, and bootcamps on everything from product strategy and business design to pitching and growth.

## The Graph

Grids are everywhere, and we wanted Grafit to feel different. So instead of scrolling, you **explore**: outfits live on an **infinite canvas** that you can wander in **any direction**. The idea is that outfits with a similar *vibe* sit close together, so the further you drift from where you started, the more new styles and niches you discover.

## Tech Stack

### App

<Stack items={["Capacitor", "Next.js", "React", "TypeScript", "SQLite"]} />

With a deadline and no mobile experience between us, [Capacitor.js](https://capacitorjs.com/) was an easy pick. As the main *frontend* developer, it let me build with the web stack I already knew, while still reaching native features like the camera, the file system and haptics.

Grafit is a **tool** first and a social app second, so it works **fully offline**, with everything stored on the device. Smart features and weather catch up once you're back online, all except background removal, which runs **on-device** with Google's [ML Kit](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/selfie-segmentation) when there's no connection.

### Backend

<Stack items={["Express", "TypeScript", "Prisma", "PostgreSQL", "nginx"]} />

Since the app owns its data, the backend is mostly a **safety net**: a copy of everything in the cloud, and the foundation for Grafit's social side. Outfits and items get their IDs on the device, so changes can pile up offline and **sync** in batches as soon as there's a connection. Users sign in with **Google**, and an nginx proxy puts the backend and *GrafitEye* behind a single API.

### *GrafitEye*

<Stack items={["Python", "FastAPI", "ONNX Runtime", "Gemini"]} />

Led by **Júlio**, *GrafitEye* is where the magic happens. Every new outfit goes through a few steps:

<Pipeline>
  <Step icon="Scissors" title="Cut out" tech="rembg" href="https://github.com/danielgatis/rembg">The person is separated from the background, so the outfit view can focus on them.</Step>
  <Step icon="MagnifyingGlass" title="Spot" tech="FashionFail" href="https://github.com/rizavelioglu/fashionfail">Each piece of clothing is found and outlined, then sorted into tops, bottoms, outerwear, footwear or accessories.</Step>
  <Step icon="PushPin" title="Pin">The center of each item gives us its color and its exact spot on the photo, and the item is added to the wardrobe.</Step>
  <Step icon="CloudSun" title="Forecast" tech="Open-Meteo" href="https://open-meteo.com/">The weather at that place and time is attached, even for outfits added offline days before.</Step>
  <Step icon="Sparkle" title="Vibe check" tech="Gemini 2.5 Flash" soon>The outfit gets a name and a score for each of its aesthetics, like *streetwear* or *cozy*, so the graph can group outfits with a similar vibe.</Step>
</Pipeline>
