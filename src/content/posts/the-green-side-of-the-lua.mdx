Lua is small, fast and loved: it has ranked among the four fastest-growing languages on GitHub. Yet in the most cited ranking of programming languages by **energy efficiency**, it places **23rd out of 27**, using almost **46× more energy than C**.

But that ranking only measured a single version of Lua's interpreter. So, together with <Person username="brandao07" name="André Brandão" />, <Person username="miguel-amg" name="Miguel Guimarães" />, <Person username="simaocunha71" name="Simão Cunha" /> and <Person username="joaoSaraiva" name="João Saraiva" />, we asked a simple question: what happens when you measure *all* of it?

The answer became *The Green Side of the Lua*, which I presented at [SANER 2026](https://conf.researchr.org/home/saner-2026), and an extended version that's currently under review for a journal. The full talk is at the end of this post!

## How We Measured

<Stack items={["Lua", "LuaJIT", "C", "Intel RAPL", "GPS-UP"]} />

<Pipeline>
  <Step icon="Stack" title="Line up the contenders" tech="LuaJIT" href="https://luajit.org/">All 17 official Lua interpreters, from 5.3.0 to 5.5.0, plus 7 LuaJIT releases and the LuaJIT Remake project.</Step>
  <Step icon="Code" title="Pick the workload" tech="LuaAOT benchmarks" href="https://github.com/hugomg/lua-aot-5.4">Seven classic programs, like *binary-trees*, *n-body* and *spectral-norm*, with equivalent C versions as a baseline.</Step>
  <Step icon="Lightning" title="Measure the energy" tech="Intel RAPL">Each program runs 10 times while the CPU reports the energy used by the processor and the memory.</Step>
  <Step icon="Thermometer" title="Keep it fair">Before every run, the CPU has to cool back down to its idle temperature, and outliers are filtered out.</Step>
  <Step icon="ChartLine" title="Score it" tech="GPS-UP">Speedup, Greenup and Powerup tell us how much faster and greener each version is, and whether it draws more power to get there.</Step>
</Pipeline>

## What We Found

<Features>
  <Feature icon="Leaf" title="Newer is greener">Energy use trended down across releases, with the biggest drop at Lua 5.4.0, which cut it by a third. Lua 5.4.7 is the greenest interpreter of all.</Feature>
  <Feature icon="Timer" title="Time is energy">Runtime and energy are almost perfectly correlated, so the fastest versions were also the greenest.</Feature>
  <Feature icon="Lightning" title="JIT changes everything">Every LuaJIT release beat every interpreter. The best, LuaJIT 2.0.4, used about 7× less energy and ran 7× faster than Lua 5.4.7.</Feature>
</Features>

## Climbing the Ranking

Back to that energy ranking. With LuaJIT 2.0.4, Lua needs only **5.63× the energy** of C and runs **7.85× slower**, instead of the 38.04× and 54.74× of Lua 5.4.7. That's enough to climb past several languages:

<Stats items={[{ value: "+5", label: "places in energy with LuaJIT" }, { value: "+9", label: "places in runtime with LuaJIT" }]} />

It's an indicative comparison, since the original ranking was measured on different hardware, but the message is clear: Lua's poor standing comes from the interpreter, not the language.

## Running on Empty

Electric cars are rated by how far they can go on a full charge, so we did the same with a laptop: charge it to 100%, run the same program in a loop until the battery dies, and count. If every run were a kilometer, C would drive 604 km, LuaJIT 109 km, and the Lua interpreter just 19 km.

## The Extended Version

The conference paper was just the beginning. In the extended version, where I share first authorship with **André** and **Miguel**, we took the study a lot further:

<Stats items={[{ value: "26", label: "Lua environments" }, { value: "30", label: "programs" }, { value: "5", label: "benchmark suites" }]} />

Besides Lua 5.5.0 and LuaJIT 2.1, we added a third way of running Lua: **ahead-of-time** compilation, with LuaAOT. And since speed-tuned benchmarks don't look much like everyday Lua, we added four suites built on popular Lua libraries, covering linear algebra, functional pipelines, data structures and object-oriented code:

<Stack items={["LuaMatrix", "LuaFun", "Penlight Collections", "Penlight OO Classes"]} />

Measuring everything took over 11 hours of execution, and more than a day once you count the cool-downs between runs. Here's what came out of it:

<Features columns={2}>
  <Feature icon="Trophy" title="LuaJIT 2.1 takes the crown">Across all 30 programs, it ran 6.3× faster and used 6.6× less energy than Lua 5.4.7.</Feature>
  <Feature icon="Path" title="AOT is a middle ground">LuaAOT was 1.5× faster and greener than the best interpreter, but still about 4× behind LuaJIT 2.1.</Feature>
  <Feature icon="FunnelSimple" title="No free fusion">Not even LuaJIT merges chained *map* and *filter* calls into a single pass, so fusing them by hand is still the fastest option.</Feature>
  <Feature icon="Cube" title="Objects level the field">On object-oriented code, the gap shrinks: JIT helps a lot less when every call has to walk a chain of metatables.</Feature>
</Features>

> The poor standing of Lua in global energy rankings is largely an artifact of evaluating legacy interpreters.

Every chart and table is in its [online appendix](https://the-green-side-of-the-lua.wasmer.app/).
