## A Quick Tour

### The Dashboard

Everything starts at the dashboard, where you can search, filter and add resources, and get anywhere else in a click. The *Popular* and *Newest* feeds sit in the sidebar, right next to your favorites.

![The dashboard](/assets/unishare/full_page.webp)

### Finding Resources

The search bar looks through every field of a resource, from its title, description and hashtags to its course, subject, type, author and even file format. Filters narrow it down by type, course or subject, each with its own search, which comes in handy with hundreds of courses to choose from.

### Sharing a Resource

Sharing takes a single dialog: upload the file, give it a title, a type, a course and a subject, and optionally a description and some hashtags. Admins can even add new types, courses and subjects right from it.

![The dialog to share a new resource](/assets/unishare/resource_dialog.webp)

### Discussing It

Every resource has its own page, with a preview of the file for PDFs and images, right next to its comments, where you can join the discussion or just leave a thank you.

![The resource page, with its comments next to a preview of the file](/assets/unishare/resourcepage.webp)

### Profiles

Click anyone's name to open their profile, with everything they've shared, favorited, commented on and upvoted. On your own profile, you can also edit your details and account settings.

![A user's profile](/assets/unishare/profile.webp)

## What Makes a Resource Popular

The *Popular* feed ranks resources by a score that adds up everything people do with them. Not every interaction is worth the same, though: an upvote says a lot more than a download, and downvotes count against it.

## Under the Hood

<Stack items={["Next.js", "TypeScript", "Tailwind CSS", "shadcn/ui", "MongoDB", "NextAuth.js", "Docker"]} />

UniShare is a single **Next.js** app that serves both the pages and a REST API, backed by **MongoDB**. Users sign in with NextAuth.js, using an email and a password (hashed with bcrypt) or their GitHub account. Every list is paginated by the API itself, so only the current page is ever fetched, and two scripts can export or import a snapshot of the whole platform, database and files included, to keep backups.

It's also fully **containerized**: a single `docker compose up` starts the app and its MongoDB database, each in its own container, with the data kept in a volume between runs.

To make the course and subject filters useful from day one, we also wrote a scraper for the University of Minho's course catalog, which filled the database with every course and its subjects:
